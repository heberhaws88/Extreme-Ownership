That's what we're talking about tonight. Chapter twelve of Extreme Ownership by Jocko Willink and Leif Babin. Discipline equals freedom.

And the core idea is this: the very things that feel like they're boxing you in, that feel restrictive, that feel like constraints on your time and your choices — routine, structure, self-imposed standards — those are exactly what create the space and the margin and the capability to handle whatever comes at you. The guy who thinks he's free because he has no alarm, no plan, no structure — he's the one who's actually trapped. Because when something unexpected hits, when someone needs him, when an opportunity shows up, he's got no bandwidth. He's already buried. The disciplined guy, the guy who looks rigid from the outside, he's the one who can flex. He's the one who's actually free.

And if I'm honest, this is the lens where I've failed at this principle the most. Where I've told myself I had freedom when really I was just avoiding the discipline that would actually set me free.

Let me take a minute here to tie this to the Savior, because if we're talking about discipline equaling freedom in the context of discipleship, we have to start with Him.

Jesus Christ is the most free being who ever lived. Perfect love, perfect truth, perfect power. Completely free. And He is also the most disciplined. Every choice He made was intentional. Every moment ordered toward the Father's will. He didn't wander. He didn't improvise His way through mortality hoping it would all work out. He was up early. He prayed. He fasted. He knew the scriptures cold — not just knew them, lived them. When the adversary tried to trap Him in the wilderness, the Savior didn't have to go scramble for an answer. He had the discipline already built in. The word was already in Him.

And then Gethsemane. The most extreme moment of ownership in all of history. The Father gave Him a choice. He could walk away. And what does He say? Not my will, but thine be done. That's discipline. That's submission to a higher order, to a standard outside Himself, even when everything in His mortal body was screaming to stop. And that discipline, that submission, is what made the Atonement possible. It's what made our freedom possible.

He owned something that wasn't His fault. He took the weight of every sin, every failure, every broken thing — none of it His doing — and He said, I'll carry it. That's the ultimate picture of extreme ownership. And it was made possible by a life of absolute discipline.

The times I've been least free in my walk as a disciple are the times I've let the discipline slide. The times I've told myself, I don't need to read my scriptures tonight, I'm too tired. And because I had no discipline, I had no foundation. So when life got hard — and it did get hard, it got really hard that year — I had nothing to stand on. My business was struggling. I was buried. And I kept telling myself I was too busy to do anything about it. I was too busy to read. Too busy to pray, really pray. Too busy to go to the temple more than once every couple months. I needed freedom, I thought. I needed space. I needed less on my plate.

What I actually needed was more discipline.

My wife saw it before I did. One night, after the kids were in bed, she just looked at me and said, When's the last time you really prayed? Not a quick prayer. A real one. That was the kickoff point. I realized I'd been lying to myself. I'd been telling myself I was too busy for discipline, when really I was just avoiding it. Because discipline is hard. Structure is hard. It's easier to be reactive, to just deal with whatever's in front of you in the moment, to tell yourself you'll get to the important stuff later.

But later never comes. And without the discipline, I wasn't free. I was trapped. Trapped in my own head. Trapped in worry. Trapped in reactivity. I couldn't lead my family spiritually because I wasn't leading myself spiritually. I couldn't help that soldier because I had no bandwidth. If I wanted margin in my life, if I wanted clarity, if I wanted to actually be able to lead and help and make good decisions, I needed the foundation. And the foundation was the discipline.

The discipline gave me freedom. The structure gave me margin. The routine that felt restrictive in the moment was actually what created the space for everything else.
