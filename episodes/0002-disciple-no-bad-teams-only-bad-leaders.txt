This is chapter two of Jocko Willink and Leif Babin's book Extreme Ownership, and the core idea is simple and brutal. When a team is struggling, when performance is poor, when standards are slipping, the problem is not the team. The problem is the leader. The team's attitude, effort, and output are a direct reflection of what the leader sets as the standard, what the leader models, what the leader is willing to tolerate. A failing team is a leadership failure first. Always.

Jocko tells the story in the book about observing SEAL training, watching boat crew races where one crew consistently came in last and another consistently won. The leadership swapped the leaders of the two crews. The winning leader took over the losing crew. Within a few iterations, that crew started winning. Same guys. Different leader. The personnel didn't change. The leadership did. That's the principle.

And tonight I want to look at this through the lens of discipleship, because if you're trying to follow Jesus Christ, this principle cuts deeper than anywhere else. This is about more than managing a team at work or leading a squad in the field. This is about the people you're responsible for spiritually, emotionally, eternally — your family, your calling in the church, the people the Lord has put in your care. And the standard for leadership in the kingdom of God is higher than anywhere else, because the leader we're trying to follow didn't just set a high bar. He took ownership that wasn't even His to take.

Let me connect that before I go further.

Jesus Christ is the only being who has ever walked this earth with zero responsibility for the mess we're in. He didn't sin. He didn't fall. He didn't contribute to the problem. And yet in the Garden of Gethsemane, He took full ownership of all of it. Every failure, every broken covenant, every bit of suffering and consequence that came from choices He didn't make. That's the Atonement. He looked at a team — humanity — that was utterly failing, and instead of saying, "Not my fault, not my problem," He said, "I'll take it. All of it. I'll lead them out."

That's ownership at a level we can't fully comprehend. But it's the pattern. He didn't blame us. He didn't write us off as bad personnel. He led.

The leader sets the standard. The leader goes first. The leader doesn't ask anything of the team that he isn't willing to do himself.

When Peter denied Him three times, Jesus didn't cut him loose. He came back after the resurrection and said, "Feed my sheep." He saw Peter's failure, and He led him through it.

That's the lens tonight.
