The principle we're working with tonight comes from chapter five of Extreme Ownership, the book by Jocko Willink and Leif Babin. It's called Cover and Move. The core idea is simple but it cuts deep: every part of a team exists to support every other part. You don't win by having the best individual performers doing their own thing in their own lanes. You win when everyone understands that their success is tied to everyone else's success, and they act like it. When one element of the team is pinned down or struggling, the other elements move to support them. Not because it's their job description. Because that's how teams actually win.

In a SEAL platoon, that's literal — one squad provides suppressive fire so another squad can advance. In an electrical contracting business, it looks different, but the principle is exactly the same. If one of my crews is drowning and I've got another crew that finished early, and I don't move guys over to help, I'm not running a team. I'm running three separate operations that happen to share a logo.

I left my A-team grinding on the medical office, working late, coming in on Saturdays, trying to make up time and deal with all the changes, getting more and more burned out. I told myself I was keeping things simple. Stable crews, consistent job assignments, nobody bouncing around.
